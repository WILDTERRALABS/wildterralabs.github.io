QuoteReady Privacy Policy

WildTerra Labs LLC — Effective: October 15, 2026 · Last updated: October 17, 2026

QuoteReady

About this policy

QuoteReady is an Android app for landscaping contractors that helps you turn a site walk into a priced quote. It is made by WildTerra Labs LLC (Washington State, USA). Questions about this policy: privacy@wildterralabs.com.

The short version

  • Your jobs, customers, invoices, payments, photos and prices stay on your phone, and, if Android's backup is on, in your own Google account's backup (see "Backups"). There are no accounts with us. We don't receive your quotes and can't see them.
  • Nothing leaves your phone unless you tap "Suggest more with AI" and confirm. Then the details listed below are sent to our server, which passes them to Anthropic (the company behind the Claude AI model) to produce suggested quantities. Prices are never sent.
  • No ads, no tracking, and we don't sell your data. QuoteReady shows no advertising, has no advertising or analytics software, and has no crash reporting of our own. The one exception is outside our control: if you use "Measure with camera", Google's ARCore component collects some usage and diagnostic information of its own (see "Measuring with the camera").
  • Tracing an area with the camera happens on your phone. If you tap inside a bed or lawn in "Measure with camera" and QuoteReady traces its edge, the camera picture is analysed on your phone and is never stored or sent.
  • Measuring with the camera runs on your phone. QuoteReady doesn't record, keep or send the camera image, only the points you tap.

What stays on your phone

Everything you enter is stored only in the app's private storage on your device:

  • your business profile (business name, your name, phone, email, address, license number, logo, default markup and tax settings, standard terms);
  • the measurements you take, including ones made with the camera, and the sketches of measured outlines you choose to save with your job photos;
  • your price list, including any prices you import from a CSV file you choose (the file is read on your phone and is never uploaded);
  • your customers (name, phone, email, address);
  • your jobs (job name, job-site address, notes, measurements, answers, photos), their status (draft, sent, accepted or declined) and the estimates and quotes built from them;
  • your jobs and their visits (the customer and job-site address, the days and times, and the notes and photos you add when you complete a visit), which are kept on the phone like your quotes;
  • your invoices (made from accepted quotes, with their lines and prices, dates, payment terms and notes) and the payments you record on them (amount, date, how it was paid, and a note such as a check number). QuoteReady takes no payments: these are your own records of money you received;
  • the "How to pay" note you write for your invoices (for example who checks are payable to, or a Zelle number);
  • any quote templates you save (item names, units and quantities; no prices or customer details);
  • any follow-up reminders and overdue-invoice reminders you set (see "Reminders" below).

We never receive this data. It is removed from the phone when you delete a job in the app or uninstall QuoteReady. If Android's backup is on, a copy of part of it is also kept in your own Google account; see "Backups" for exactly what, and how to turn it off.

The one thing that leaves your phone: AI suggestions

The "Suggest more with AI" button on an estimate is optional. It needs an internet connection and sends a request only after you tap it and confirm in the dialog. The request contains:

  • The job description text shown in the dialog. It starts as your scope-of-work text and job notes, and you can edit it before sending. It is sent exactly as typed, so please don't type things you don't want sent.
  • The trade (landscaping) and the measurements you entered for the job (label, value, unit).
  • Your answers to the on-screen questions for that job (for example areas, depths, and yes/no choices).
  • The names, units and categories of the items in your price list (for example "Mulch, cu yd, material"). Never your prices, costs or markup.
  • The lines already in the estimate (description, unit and quantity, with no prices).
  • Up to 4 job photos, only if you tick "Include up to 4 photos" for that request. They are JPEGs reduced on your phone to at most 1,600 pixels on the long edge.
  • A random install ID the app created the first time it ran, and an app token. They are used to limit how much any one install can use the service. The install ID is not linked to your name, your email, or any account.
  • A Play Integrity token, if your phone can make one. Google generates it to show this is the genuine QuoteReady installed from Google Play on a genuine, unmodified device, and it is tied to this one request. Our server sends it to Google to be checked and does not keep it. It does not contain your name, email or any of your job details.
  • If you have a QuoteReady Pro subscription, your Google Play purchase token. It is a code Google gives the app when you subscribe. Our server uses it to ask Google whether your subscription is active (so subscribers get higher AI limits), and it is sent only with AI requests. It does not contain your name, email or payment details.

Not sent: your customers' names, phone numbers, email addresses or addresses (as entered in the customer fields), or a job's site address; your business name, logo, or contact details; any prices; your quote PDFs; or anything about invoices (the invoices themselves, the payments you record, what a customer owes, or your "How to pay" note) or about jobs and visits (the schedule, visit notes and photos, or job-site addresses).

Who handles an AI request

Our server. It receives the request, forwards it to Anthropic, and returns the suggestions to your phone. It does not store the notes, measurements, answers, photos or suggestions. It keeps only short-lived counters used for rate limiting: how many requests an install ID has made this hour and today (these expire within two days), our total spend for the day (which contains no personal information), and, for subscribers, Google's answer about whether a subscription is active, kept for up to an hour under a one-way hash of the purchase token (we don't store the token itself). Our server's own logs record technical facts about each request (which AI model, how many tokens, the cost, how long it took) and never the content of a request or the install ID. Google receives the purchase token and the Play Integrity token from our server, where those checks are switched on, to confirm the subscription and that the request comes from the genuine app (using Google Play's developer and integrity APIs).

Railway (railway.com) hosts our server in the United States and acts as our service provider. Railway keeps its own request log for our service. It records the IP address each request came from, with the time, the address requested and the response, but not the content of the request. Railway keeps those logs for a limited time that depends on our hosting plan (between 3 and 30 days, according to Railway's documentation). We use them only to run and troubleshoot the service; our own server code does not read, log or store IP addresses. Railway's handling of its own account and usage data is described in Railway's privacy policy.

Anthropic, PBC (the Claude API). Anthropic processes the content of the request to produce the suggestions. Anthropic's Commercial Terms (section B, effective June 17, 2025) say it may not train its models on this content. According to Anthropic's retention article (last updated July 1, 2026), inputs and outputs sent through its API are automatically deleted from its backend within 30 days of receipt or generation, with these exceptions: content flagged by Anthropic's safety systems for violating its Usage Policy may be kept for up to 2 years (and the safety classification scores for up to 7 years), and data may be kept where the law requires. Anthropic's own privacy policy applies to its handling of the data it receives.

We use these providers only to deliver the feature. We don't sell personal information and we don't share it for advertising.

Our server runs in the United States. Our providers may process data in the United States and in other countries.

Measuring with the camera

On phones that support Google's ARCore, Measure with camera lets you measure on the ground: a line (its length) or an outline (its area and the distance around it). You aim the phone at the ground and tap points; the screen shows feet and square feet as you go.

What QuoteReady does. The camera is on only while the measuring screen is open. You can tap the corners of an outline yourself, or tap inside a bed or lawn and QuoteReady traces its edge for you to check and adjust (drag the dots, add or remove points). To trace an edge, QuoteReady takes one camera picture at the moment you tap and analyses it on your phone, using a small model that is part of the app (Google's open-source MediaPipe, with a model file inside the app); it needs no internet connection and sends nothing anywhere. Camera pictures are processed on your device and are never stored or sent: the picture is held in memory only long enough to find the edge, then discarded. It is not saved to your phone's storage, it is not added to your job photos, and it is not sent to us, to Anthropic or to anyone else. It is also never part of what the "Suggest more with AI" button sends. What QuoteReady keeps is only the points of the outline (which you can adjust), turned into the measurements you choose to keep (with the label you give them), and, if you choose, a drawn sketch of an outline (lines and numbers, not the camera picture) saved with your job photos. Nothing about a measurement is sent to us. The first time, Android asks you to allow the camera, and QuoteReady shows Google's required notice that the feature runs on Google Play Services for AR.

What Google does. This feature runs on Google Play Services for AR (ARCore), which is provided by Google LLC and governed by the Google Privacy Policy. Google may install or update it on your phone the first time you measure. According to Google's ARCore data safety page (last updated by Google on October 31, 2024; checked on October 15, 2026), for the features QuoteReady uses ARCore always collects, as a required part of ARCore, user identifiers (Google account identifiers if you are signed in) or device identifiers, performance and diagnostic data, and API usage and app activity (which ARCore calls an app makes), for analytics and to keep and improve ARCore; Google says it does not share these with third parties and encrypts them in transit. QuoteReady does not receive any of it. QuoteReady does not use ARCore's Cloud Anchors, Geospatial or video features, which Google says collect location or video data, and the app has no location permission. If you don't use "Measure with camera", ARCore is never started.

Camera measuring is an estimate: it can drift over distance, and a traced edge can be wrong where the ground is hard to tell apart (lawn fading into a path, say). QuoteReady warns you when the edge looks unclear, when the area runs out of view and when points are far away. Check anything important with a tape.

Sending a quote

When you tap Send quote, QuoteReady creates a PDF and opens Android's share sheet. You choose where it goes (email, a messaging app, and so on). The PDF contains your business details, your customer's name and contact details if you entered them, the line items and prices, and your site photos if you chose to include them. That transfer is between you and the app you pick. We don't receive it. Make sure you're comfortable sharing your customer's details this way, and that you have their permission where the law requires it.

Jobs, visits and the calendar

Jobs and visits are stored on your phone only, in the same way as quotes: nothing about them is sent to us or to anyone else, and they are never part of an AI request. Photos you take or add when completing a visit are kept in the app's private photo folder with your quote photos.

Navigate. The Navigate button hands a job-site address to the maps app on your phone (Google Maps, usually). That is you choosing to open that app on that address; the app that opens has its own privacy policy and may see the address, and QuoteReady never learns where your phone is: it has no location permission. If no maps app can be opened, the address is copied instead.

Sending an invoice

When you tap Send invoice, QuoteReady creates a PDF and opens Android's share sheet, exactly as for a quote. The PDF contains your business details, your customer's name and contact details if you entered them, the work and prices, the payments you've recorded, the balance due, and your "How to pay" note. That transfer is between you and the app you pick. We don't receive it. Invoices and payments are never part of an AI request.

Backups

Backup and restore are in the app under Business > Backup & restore, and are entirely your choice.

  • Back up now packs everything listed above (your profile and logo, price list, customers, quotes and their estimates, jobs and visits, invoices and their payments, templates, and the photos on your quotes and visits) into a single zip file on your phone and opens Android's share sheet. You decide where it goes (Google Drive, email, a messaging app, another phone). We never receive it. The file is not encrypted and contains your customers' details and your prices, so keep it somewhere private.
  • Restore reads a backup file you choose with Android's own file picker and replaces what's on the phone with it, after showing you what's in the backup and asking you to confirm.
  • Android's automatic backup is on for QuoteReady, so a new phone doesn't depend on you remembering to make a file. When Android's backup is turned on on your phone (Android Settings, Google, Backup), Android itself copies part of QuoteReady's data to your own Google account, and puts it back when you reinstall QuoteReady or set up a new phone with that account. What it copies: the app's database (your business profile, price list, customers and their contact details, quotes and estimates, jobs and visits, invoices and the payments you recorded, templates, and the file locations of your photos), and a small settings file (which quotes have used a free slot, and the date of your last backup file). It does not copy your photos or logo, the random install ID, anything about your subscription, or reminders. That copy is made by Android and kept by Google; we never receive it and can't see it. Per Android's documentation, it is a private copy in your Google Drive that doesn't count against your storage, only the latest copy is kept, and on Android 9 and newer it is end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock if you have set one. Google's own terms and privacy policy apply to it, and Google's backup help describes how backups are managed.
  • Moving straight from an old phone to a new one (cable or wireless setup) also carries your photos and logo. On Android 11 and older that transfer follows the cloud rules above, so photos only move with a backup file.
  • To stop it: turn off backup in Android Settings (Google, Backup; the wording varies by phone). According to Google's help, turning backup off deletes your existing backups, and a phone's backup is also erased if the phone isn't used for 57 days. Uninstalling QuoteReady doesn't by itself delete the copy in your Google account.

Reminders

There are three kinds, and all are optional and off unless you turn them on: a follow-up on a quote you've sent, an overdue reminder on an invoice you've sent, and a morning list of the day's visits.

If you choose a follow-up reminder for a quote you've sent, QuoteReady asks Android to show a notification on your phone a set number of days later (at 9:00 in the morning). Everything about it happens on your phone: the notification's text is made on the device from the quote (the quote number, your customer's name and the job name, for example "Check in with Dana about Spring cleanup"), handed to Android's own notification system, and shown by Android. Nothing about a reminder is sent to us or to anyone else. Reminders are off unless you turn them on. A reminder is cancelled when you mark the quote accepted or declined, move it back to draft, or delete it.

An overdue reminder works the same way. It's shown at 9:00, a number of days after the invoice's due date, only if the invoice still has a balance, and its text (the invoice number, your customer's name and the amount they still owe, for example "Dana Ruiz still owes $362.16 for Front beds") is made on your phone and shown by Android. It is cancelled when the invoice is paid, taken back to draft or deleted. Nothing about it is sent to us or to anyone else.

The morning list is a notification at the hour you choose (6 to 9 AM) on days that have visits, such as "Today: 3 visits" with a line for each: the time, the customer's name and the job. It never includes an address. It is made on your phone from your calendar and shown by Android; nothing about it is sent to us.

Because a reminder names your customer (and, for an invoice, what they owe), it can appear on your lock screen. You can hide notification contents on the lock screen in Android's settings.

Subscriptions

QuoteReady Pro is a subscription sold through Google Play. Google handles the purchase and your payment details; we never see your card or payment information. The first three jobs are free (a quote and its first invoice are one job; each further invoice for the same job counts as another job). The app checks with the Google Play app on your phone whether you're subscribed (when it opens and when you come back to it), and remembers the answer on your phone so it keeps working offline for a while. Manage or cancel your subscription in Google Play. Google's own privacy policy applies to what it collects when you buy.

Links in the app

QuoteReady has links to this Privacy Policy and to our Terms of Use, on the More tab and on the subscription screen. Nothing is opened unless you tap one. A link opens in your phone's browser, and the page is hosted by GitHub (GitHub Pages), which, like any website, can see your IP address and basic browser details when your browser loads the page. QuoteReady itself sends nothing.

Permissions

  • Internet, used only for the optional AI feature.
  • Google Play billing (com.android.vending.BILLING) and network state, added by Google's billing library so the app can offer and check the subscription.
  • Notifications (Android asks you the first time you turn on a follow-up, overdue or morning-list reminder, or once when you open the app on a phone whose restored quotes or invoices already have reminders), used only to show those reminders. If you say no, the app still marks the quote "Follow up" in your list when the day comes; you just don't get the notification.
  • Run at startup (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED), used only so reminders you've already set aren't lost when the phone restarts. Vibrate, used by the notification, as with most apps that show one.
  • Camera (CAMERA): Android asks the first time you use "Measure with camera", and the first time you take a photo with the in-app Camera button. It is used only for those, never in the background. In "Measure with camera" the picture is used on your phone to trace an edge and is never stored or sent.
  • Photos and files: QuoteReady uses Android's own photo picker and file picker. It does not hold any storage permission, and when you import a price list it can read only the one file you choose.
  • Google Play Services for AR, which Google may install the first time you use camera measuring (it is Google's component, not part of QuoteReady).
  • Google MediaPipe, open-source software inside QuoteReady (with a small model file) that finds the edge of the bed or lawn you tap in "Measure with camera". It runs entirely on your phone, has no network use of its own in this app, and is given nothing but the camera picture for that moment. Its licence notices are under More > Open-source licenses.

The app has no accounts, location tracking, contacts access, advertising ID use, or analytics or crash reporting of its own. (Google's ARCore component, used only for camera measuring, collects some usage and diagnostic information of its own; see "Measuring with the camera".)

Your choices

  • Don't use the AI button and nothing ever leaves your phone (apart from what Google's ARCore collects if you use "Measure with camera"; type your measurements instead and ARCore is never started).
  • Delete your data on the device by deleting a job (and its photos) in the app, or by uninstalling. If Android's backup was on, a copy may also be in your Google account until you remove it there.
  • Turn off Android's backup for your phone in Android Settings; per Google, that also deletes the existing backups.
  • AI requests: we don't keep them, so there is nothing of ours to delete. Anthropic's handling is as described above. To ask about anything tied to your install ID, or to ask us any privacy question, contact privacy@wildterralabs.com.
  • Depending on where you live (for example the EU/UK under GDPR, or California under the CCPA/CPRA) you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or port your personal data, and to object to or restrict its processing. Because we hold no accounts and keep no request content, there is generally nothing for us to retrieve, but you are welcome to ask, and we will respond within the time the law requires.

Security

Requests to our server use HTTPS (Android blocks plain HTTP for release apps). The data on your phone relies on Android's own protections, such as your screen lock and device encryption. No system is perfectly secure.

Children

QuoteReady is a business tool and is not directed to children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If we change what the app sends or who handles it, we will update this page and the "Last updated" date above before the change ships. If you keep using the AI feature after an update, the updated policy applies.
